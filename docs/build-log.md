First real Muse run (model sheets for all four characters, a three-panel strip, every panel passed self-review) found four gaps, now fixed: the API takes only 1024x1024, 1536x1024 and 1024x1536, so 4:5 panels are requested at 1024x1536 and centre-cropped; Muse answers in WebP, which the SVG renderer cannot embed, so every image is normalised to PNG; prompts refused by the content filter are retried once with gentler wording and a refused test panel no longer stops a sheet run; tests run on Windows with Node 20. Lettering redone after review of that strip ("the bubbles don't fit, the font isn't comic"): hand-lettered comic font with full Vietnamese (Mali Bold, OFL; Bangers for shouts), hand-drawn balloons by kind (speech with a short curved tail, thought cloud with puffs, spiky shout, dashed whisper) chosen by a new `kind` on each script line, bubbles sized to their words, and panel prompts that forbid drawn thought clouds. Second review: "text must read on a phone; three panels on one page make it too small", so every panel now posts as its own 1080x1350 image (strip3 included) and lettering is sized for a phone, about 17 pt with 14 pt flagged as overflow. The letter step brings every panel to 1080x1350 first: near-4:5 panels are centre-cropped, and an old wide panel is shown whole over a blurred copy of itself. Third review: "the backgrounds are too dark and gloomy", so the series style, the storyboard schema and every panel prompt now ask for cheerful lighting that fits the scene: daytime by default, evening or night only when the story needs it and then warm and lively, with gloomy lighting on the avoid list; tension comes from poses and faces, not lighting. Office floor plan (`series/office.yaml`, `studio office` draws `series/office-plan.png`): a fixed seat for each character, windows, meeting room, pantry and the boss's office in fixed places, and background animal coworkers at their own desks. The storyboard writer gets the whole plan, and each panel prompt gets the room, the seats of the characters on stage and the rule to fill the background with coworkers working. Page art (`studio brand-art`): a profile picture (1024x1024, readable in a small circle) and a Facebook cover (1640x624, action kept in the band that survives the phone crop) for every brand in channels.yaml. The series cover is an indoor homage to a famous album-cover crosswalk shot, with the four coworkers crossing a zebra crossing painted on the office aisle; each character's cover has that character crossing alone while the office watches. A second cover has the page name and tagline lettered in the comic font. Image quality fix after Tài found the art soft: the `size` sent to Muse only picks the aspect ratio, and Muse draws at about 2.5 MP (1280x1920 for a tall frame), but every result was being shrunk to the requested 1024 px and returned as lossy WebP. Now images come back as PNG, are cropped to shape at full resolution, and are scaled once, down, to the post size. Fourth art review: "the pose sheet plays it safe; comics should be more extra, with more exaggerated drawing". The series style now asks for pushed cartoon acting (squash and stretch, extreme faces, strong lines of action) and allows drawn comic effects such as sweat drops, motion lines, shock lines, anger veins and steam, as pictures and never as letters. The pose sheet grew from six calm poses to twelve extreme ones in a 4x3 grid (melting off the chair, leaping in shock, fist pump, sneaking, sprinting late, squashed under paperwork and more), sheet expressions are pushed to extremes, and the storyboard and panel prompts ask for the same acting. After reading every Larkpulse feature page (comparison in the project doc), characters talk back: `story replies` drafts answers to new comments in the voice of each episode's lead and `story first-comment` opens every new post with the act's audience question; both wait in `data/replies.json` for approval and `channel send-replies` posts them from the account that published the post. `channel insights` pulls reactions, shares, saves and reach per post for the coach. Coach lessons now wait for the editor's approval before the writer uses them. Safety, after Tài asked to keep harmful content and other people's scripts out: one banned-topic list (`series/safety.yaml`) read by the comment screen, the scorer, the writer, a new `safety` step that gates the script before drawing and the panel review; prompt-injection rules; `studio episode approve` hashes every file and the post, and `channel publish` refuses anything not approved as it is; a provenance log per episode; CODEOWNERS (docs/safety.md). |
